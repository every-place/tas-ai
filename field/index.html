Guided inspection of a toilet stall

This shows how the app could lead an inspector from the room to a finished Finding. The inspector picks the room, the element and its type, and the app lists what TAS requires. The inspector scans the stall, the measurements appear on the image, and the app flags each check.

Example: a renovated cafe, too small for a pre-visit. The inspector names locations on the spot, and the app suggests the next free code, such as TC01 for the first stall or TR02 for a second restroom. A custom code can be typed instead.

The scan builds the checklist. It finds the water closet, grab bars, dispenser and door, then lists every TAS check for that stall type. Findings can't be made until every check is measured, answered, or marked not applicable, so nothing gets forgotten.

A measurement too close to its limit for the scan to be sure is marked Check with tape. When the inspector enters the tape or laser reading, the app takes a photo stamped with the location, the reading, the requirement and the TAS section. Failures become Findings. Each one offers Recommendation options from the library, a custom Recommendation with notes, or a choice to defer.

One Recommendation can settle others. Choose "replace with a wall-hung water closet" for the low seat, and the depth Finding is marked covered, because a wall-hung model needs only 56". The dispenser Finding is flagged for a re-check after installation. The report then gets one combined Recommendation that lists what the new fixture must meet. The RAS can still handle any covered Finding separately.

Photos are real. Each check has a Take photo button that opens the camera, and a From library button for photos already taken. Every photo is stamped with the site, location, reading, requirement, TAS section and time. There is no limit per check. Photos stay on this phone, even if the app is closed, until you tap Start over. Tap a photo to view it full size, save it to Photos or delete it.

The scan is simulated, the measurements are invented, and the Recommendation wording is placeholder text until Ken's library replaces it. The TAS section numbers and required values are taken from the 2012 TAS, chapter 6.
